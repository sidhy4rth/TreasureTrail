## Why invasive species are on the list

Lantana, Parthenium and yellow bells are worth points on purpose. Spotting them is useful field work: every geotagged invasive find tells the CoE where those plants are spreading on campus. The projector counts them as "invasive species reported to the CoE".

## Checking the names against the AI

The plant-ID AI only gives instant points when its answer matches a species' scientific name (or one of its accepted alternative names). Botanists rename plants often, so before the first event every species was tested against Pl@ntNet using reference photos from Wikimedia Commons:

- **46 of 57** were recognised straight away from a single reference photo.
- **3 naming gaps were fixed:** the pink trumpet tree is often returned as *Tabebuia pallida*; the areca palm was renamed *Chrysalidocarpus lutescens*; and *Ficus microcarpa* (Indian laurel fig), which the AI kept finding where people expect a banyan, was added as its own species.
- The remaining misses came from poor reference photos (night shots, herbarium scans). Close-up photos of fresh leaves score far higher, and anything doubtful goes to the judges anyway.

No numbers on this page are invented: there are no carbon or oxygen figures, only qualitative tags.
